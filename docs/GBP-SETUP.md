## 7. Owner-seeded questions and answers

Post these from the owner account under the Q&A section as soon as the profile is live. They answer the questions the research found people actually search. Each answer is under 300 characters. Check the Q&A section weekly for public questions and answer within two days.

1. **Q: How much does a mortgage broker cost?**
   A: On most mortgages, nothing. The lender pays the broker on A-lender deals. Fees arise only on some B-lender or private files, and BC rules require that they be disclosed to you in writing before you commit.

2. **Q: How much suite income will a lender count?**
   A: It depends on the lender and the suite. A legal, registered suite can count under an offset or add-back method at a share of the rent. An unregistered suite is often counted as zero. Joshua checks the lender's actual policy before you rely on it.

3. **Q: How early should I start on my mortgage renewal?**
   A: Six months before maturity is ideal; 120 days is the usual rate-hold window. Your lender's letter is one offer. Starting early leaves time to compare, and since late 2024 many switches skip the stress test if the balance and amortization do not increase.

4. **Q: Can a co-signer help me qualify?**
   A: Often, yes. A co-signer or guarantor adds their income and credit to the file and takes on real liability for the mortgage. Lenders differ on whether the co-signer must be on title. It is a serious commitment for them, so it is discussed openly first.

5. **Q: Can I buy a home with 5 percent down in BC?**
   A: Yes, on homes under $1.5 million, with mortgage insurance and the stress test. The minimum is 5 percent of the first $500,000 and 10 percent of the rest. The insurance premium is added to the mortgage. Closing costs are separate and need cash.

6. **Q: What does a real pre-approval include?**
   A: Income, credit and down payment actually checked, and a rate held for a set period, commonly up to 120 days. A quote from a website is not a pre-approval. Joshua reviews the file the way an underwriter would before a lender sees it.

7. **Q: Can I get a mortgage if I am self-employed?**
   A: Yes. Lenders read self-employed income from your tax returns, usually a two-year average, and some add back certain deductions. If the returns do not tell the full story, stated-income and B-lender options exist, with fees disclosed in advance.

8. **Q: Is switching lenders at renewal expensive?**
   A: Usually not. There is no penalty at maturity, the new lender often covers transfer costs, and since late 2024 a straight switch typically skips a new stress test. A written comparison shows whether the switch is worth it before you decide.
